2. What exactly is a Writing Drill in Writa?
I would define it as:

A short, focused, interactive writing exercise designed to develop one particular writing skill through repeated attempts, immediate feedback, guided correction and independent application.

For example, instead of asking a learner to write another complete IELTS essay to improve grammar, Writa might give them a five-minute sentence construction exercise focused on complex sentences.

The learner practises that skill several times, receives explanations and then applies it in a new context.

5. The most important part: how an individual drill should work
I would use a consistent instructional sequence across the drill library.

Understand → Attempt → Diagnose → Learn → Retry → Transfer

A drill should not simply ask a question, mark it wrong and display the correct answer.

What Writa does

Gives a new context where the learner applies the same skill independently.

This final transfer stage is particularly important. It prevents the learner from simply memorising the answer to an exercise.

Three levels of difficulty
Every drill should have three adjustable difficulty levels:

Foundation: More cues, simpler sentences, guided options.

Developing: Less support, more complex tasks.

Advanced: Open-ended, context-rich writing challenges.

The AI should adapt difficulty based on performance, not just the learner's selected IELTS target band.

9. My final product recommendation
I would make Writing Drills one of Writa's core learning modules, with three defining characteristics:

1. Skill-first, not question-first.

The learner should practise grammar, sentence construction, paragraph development and other transferable writing skills, rather than merely answering more IELTS questions.

2. Adaptive, not static.

The same drill should be able to provide different levels of guidance depending on the learner's performance.

3. Connected to actual writing.

Every drill should ultimately help the learner write better sentences, paragraphs and complete IELTS responses.

The most important design decision is to make drills short and purposeful, with a typical duration of around 3–8 minutes. A learner should be able to practise one weakness between longer writing sessions without having to commit to another complete essay.

My proposed positioning for the module:

Writing Drills — Focused practice to build the skills behind better IELTS writing.

That gives Writa a meaningful distinction from an ordinary IELTS question bank or grammar exercise application. It becomes a structured skill-development system, powered by adaptive coaching and connected to the learner's actual writing performance.

For the next design step, I would build the complete Writing Drills taxonomy and exercise catalogue, defining the exact exercise types, interaction patterns, feedback logic and Gemini behaviour for each of the eight categories.
