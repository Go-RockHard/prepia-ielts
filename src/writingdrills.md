Writa Writing Drills — Feature Architecture
My recommendation is to make Writing Drills a dedicated skill-development module within Writa, not simply a collection of short IELTS writing questions.

There is a very important distinction here:
- Writing Practice helps a candidate practise producing complete IELTS responses.
- Writing Drills help a candidate isolate, understand and improve the individual skills required to produce better responses.
Think of it this way: a candidate might be able to write a complete Task 2 essay but repeatedly struggle with complex sentences, paragraph development, paraphrasing or explaining arguments. Asking them to write another full essay may not be the most effective way to address those weaknesses.

A targeted drill lets them work on the specific problem, receive immediate feedback, repeat the skill and eventually apply it in a complete essay.

1. Do writing drills actually exist in language teaching and writing pedagogy?
Absolutely. Writing drills have a genuine pedagogical foundation, although they are not always labelled exactly as writing drills in academic literature or language courses.

Several established teaching approaches use the same underlying principles.
The established approaches behind writing drills
Pedagogical approach	What learners do	How Writa can use it
Controlled writing practice	Practise a specific language structure with limited choices.	Correct articles, verb forms, prepositions and sentence patterns.
Sentence combining	Combine short sentences into more sophisticated sentences.	Improve sentence variety and grammatical range.
Sentence expansion	Develop a basic sentence by adding meaningful detail.	Practise developing explanations and supporting ideas.
Paraphrasing practice	Express the same meaning using different vocabulary and structures.	Improve IELTS introductions and academic language.
Paragraph construction	Organise sentences into a coherent paragraph.	Develop Task 2 body paragraphs and Task 1 descriptions.
Guided composition	Construct a piece of writing with progressively less assistance.	Prepare learners for independent essay writing.
Revision and editing	Identify and improve weaknesses in existing writing.	Teach candidates to recognise and correct their own errors.

These are not just theoretical possibilities. The British Council's IELTS teaching resources include activities for sentence writing, complex sentences, sentence transformation, paragraph development, argument development and Task 1 overviews.

The Australian Education Research Organisation also recommends explicit sentence-level instruction, including sentence combining and expansion, as part of effective writing instruction. Its review draws on research involving more than 85 studies of sentence combining.

Category 2 — Sentence Construction
Highest initial priority










This should be one of Writa's flagship drill categories.

Many learners know enough vocabulary but cannot construct accurate, varied and logically connected sentences.
Drill	What it develops
Sentence Combining	Combine two or more simple sentences.
Sentence Expansion	Develop a basic sentence with additional information.
Sentence Transformation	Change sentence structures without changing meaning.
Simple to Complex	Use subordinate clauses accurately.
Complex to Simple	Make unnecessarily complicated sentences clearer.
Relative Clauses	Practise who, which, that and whose.
Conditional Sentences	Express hypothetical and conditional relationships.
Sentence Variety	Avoid repetitive sentence patterns.

Example: Sentence Combining

Given:
- Technology has improved communication.
- People can communicate instantly.
- They live in different countries.
Task: Combine these ideas into one or two well-constructed sentences.

The coach should evaluate grammatical accuracy, logical relationships and naturalness, rather than rewarding the longest sentence.

Category 4 — Coherence & Cohesion










This category develops the organisation and flow of writing.

Example: Paragraph Ordering

Give the learner four sentences:
1. As a result, many employees can work from home.
2. Technological development has transformed modern workplaces.
3. Video conferencing has made remote collaboration easier.
4. This has reduced the need for daily commuting.
The learner must arrange them into a coherent sequence.

The system should explain why the sequence works, not simply mark the answer right or wrong.
Category 5 — Idea Development & Argumentation










This is especially important for IELTS Writing Task 2.

A common problem is that candidates state an opinion but fail to explain or support it.
Drill	What it develops

Explain Your Point	Develop an argument beyond a basic statement.
Give Relevant Examples	Support claims with appropriate examples.
Cause and Effect	Explain relationships between events.

Position Development	Maintain a clear position throughout an essay.

Example: Explain Your Point

Given:
Public transport should be made cheaper.

Question: Why would cheaper public transport benefit society?

The learner must develop the idea beyond simply repeating the claim.

A useful coaching sequence:
1. What would become easier for people?
2. Which groups would benefit most?
3. How might this affect private vehicle use?
4. Can you express that relationship in one developed sentence?
This drill teaches argument development rather than memorisation of essay templates.

4. What should an individual drill session look like?
This is where I would borrow the strongest elements of Speakia's coaching philosophy.

Every drill should have a consistent instructional structure, but the interaction should change according to the drill type and learner ability.
Anatomy of a Writa drill
1. Skill introduction
   
   Briefly explain what the learner will practise and why it matters in IELTS Writing.
2. Initial challenge
   
   Give the learner an exercise without immediately showing the solution. Establish their starting ability.
3. Diagnosis
   
   Identify errors, weaknesses and areas of strength.
4. Adaptive coaching
   
   Give a hint, ask a guiding question, explain a rule or demonstrate an example as needed.
5. Focused practice
   
   Present another exercise targeting the same skill, with adjusted difficulty.
6. Independent challenge
   
   Test whether the learner can apply the skill without assistance.
7. Transfer task
   
   Ask the learner to use the skill in an authentic IELTS sentence, paragraph or response.
8. Session summary
   
   Show what improved, what remains weak and what to practise next.

Example: a complete 10-minute drill
Drill: Building Complex Sentences
Stage	Activity	Time
Introduction	Explain complex sentence construction	1 min
Initial challenge	Combine two simple sentences	1 min
Diagnosis	Identify errors and weaknesses	1 min
Coaching	Explain subordinate clauses	2 min
Focused practice	Complete three exercises	3 min
Independent challenge	Write a new sentence independently	1 min
Transfer	Apply it to an IELTS Task 2 idea	1 min

These times are illustrative, not fixed. Some drills might take three minutes; others may need fifteen.
5. How should Gemini power Writing Drills?
I would use the same hybrid AI architecture as the rest of Writa, but with a specialised Drill Coaching Engine.

Do not make every drill a realtime voice conversation. That would be unnecessary. Some drills need instant automated checking; others need deeper AI reasoning and interactive teaching.
Three interaction types

1. Instant Practice

Best for grammar correction, sentence completion, matching, ordering and multiple-choice exercises.

Use deterministic answer checking where possible, with Gemini generating explanations for mistakes or accepting valid alternatives.




2. Adaptive AI Coaching

Best for sentence construction, paraphrasing, idea development, paragraph writing and argumentation.

Use Gemini Live for interactive questions, hints, explanations and follow-up exercises.




3. Deep Writing Analysis

Best for paragraph evaluation, revision, coherence and complex writing tasks.

Use standard Gemini reasoning calls to evaluate the response against a detailed skill-specific rubric.

6. How should Writa remember and measure progress?
A drill should not be considered successful just because a candidate completes it.

Writa should measure whether the learner is actually developing the target skill.

For each skill, record:
Metric	What it tells Writa
Attempts	How frequently the learner has practised.
Accuracy	How often the learner produces correct responses.
Error frequency	Which errors keep recurring.
Hint dependency	How much assistance the learner needs.
Completion time	How efficiently the learner performs.
Independent success	Whether the learner can perform without help.
Transfer success	Whether the skill appears in actual IELTS writing.
Retention	Whether improvement persists in later practice.

I particularly recommend distinguishing between drill performance and writing performance.

For instance, a candidate may achieve 90% accuracy in grammar drills but continue making grammar mistakes in complete essays. Writa should not declare that grammar skill mastered until the learner can apply it in authentic writing.

9. My final product recommendation
There are three features I would consider essential to making Writa's Writing Drills genuinely valuable:

1. Skill-based, not question-based.

Do not organise the entire module around IELTS question types. Organise it around the skills that candidates need to develop. Task types should be a secondary filter.

2. Adaptive, not repetitive.

If the learner repeatedly makes the same mistake, the system should teach the underlying concept, change the exercise, adjust scaffolding and test independent application.

3. Transfer-oriented, not completion-oriented.

A drill is not truly successful until the learner can apply the skill in an authentic sentence, paragraph or complete IELTS response.

That is the relationship I would preserve in Writa.

One final thought: Grammar and Sentence Construction should be the first two categories you develop deeply, but don't let them dominate the entire product. IELTS writing proficiency also requires idea development, relevance, organisation and the ability to communicate a position clearly.
