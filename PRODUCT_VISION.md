# Speakia Product Vision

## Why Speakia exists

Speaking confidence grows through repeated, meaningful use of language—not by memorising polished answers or receiving a score without knowing what to do next. Yet many IELTS learners have limited access to a patient speaking partner, useful feedback, and a clear way to turn practice into progress.

Speakia aims to make deliberate speaking practice more accessible. It brings realistic practice, models, coaching, vocabulary learning, and reflection into one workspace so learners can move from understanding an example to expressing their own ideas.

Speakia is a learning aid. Its automated assessments are practice estimates, not official IELTS results or a substitute for a qualified examiner.

## The problems we solve

- **Practice can be hard to access.** Learners may not have a partner available when they are ready to speak. On-demand voice sessions and AI-to-AI demonstrations provide opportunities to practise and listen.
- **Examples can become scripts.** Model answers are useful for noticing structure and language, but rote memorisation can make speech inflexible. Speakia presents examples as adaptable starting points and gives learners ways to practise the same ideas in their own words.
- **Feedback does not always lead to action.** Broad scores can leave learners unsure what to work on. Speakia connects feedback to specific strengths, next steps, practice activities, and a reviewable history.
- **Useful language is easy to lose.** A strong phrase heard in a session may disappear when the session ends. The Lexical Vault and automatic vocabulary capture help carry useful language into later study.
- **Learning tools are often fragmented.** Practice, vocabulary, transcripts, and review can live in separate places. Speakia connects them around the learner’s speaking goals and history.

## Pedagogical principles

### Practise communication, not recitation

Learners should build the ability to respond to questions and develop ideas, rather than reproduce a fixed answer. Templates and model answers demonstrate possible structures and expressions; they are not scripts to memorise.

### Move from noticing to independent use

The experience should support a gradual sequence: explore an example, notice how it works, listen to it, practise with support, and then try again more independently. Repetition is valuable when learners can change, adapt, and personalise what they say.

### Make feedback specific and usable

Feedback should connect observations to clear next actions. It should be constructive, appropriately cautious, and grounded in available evidence. Pronunciation or delivery should not be inferred when the system has not heard the learner speak.

### Revisit language over time

Vocabulary is more likely to become usable when learners encounter it in context and retrieve it again later. Session-derived lexical resources should be saved with useful meanings, examples, or context where available, while avoiding duplicates and unsupported suggestions.

### Keep the learner in control

Learners decide what to practise, which suggestions to keep, and how to adapt examples. AI supports the learning process; it should not present uncertain evaluations as official judgments or replace the learner’s own voice.

## Intended experience

Speakia should feel like a supportive, practical study space: clear about what to do next, calm during speaking practice, and encouraging without overstating results. A learner can start with a short exercise or a complete test, choose a topic or skill, listen before speaking, and return later to review progress.

A typical learning loop is:

1. **Choose a focus** — select a speaking part, topic, skill, or saved learning resource.
2. **Explore and notice** — read or listen to a model, identify its structure, and notice useful expressions.
3. **Practise** — speak independently, with a coach, or alongside a demonstrated conversation.
4. **Reflect** — review the transcript and evidence-based feedback, then choose a manageable next step.
5. **Revisit** — save useful vocabulary and return to it through the Lexical Vault, flashcards, and later practice.

The interface should make the next meaningful action understandable while leaving room for different learning styles and practice lengths.

## How the modules fit together

- **Practice and Speaking Drills** provide realistic IELTS prompts, live examiner interaction, focused skill exercises, and opportunities to repeat an attempt.
- **AI-to-AI Simulation** lets learners listen to two AI speakers demonstrate an IELTS exchange or test format before trying it themselves.
- **Answer Builder** connects the Topic Bank to model responses, answer structures, and useful vocabulary. Its Part 1, Part 2, Part 3, and full-test prompts stay in sync with Vocabulary Studio, including custom prompts; learners can listen to AI-to-AI simulations, practise live with a candidate-to-AI examiner, review session types in the archive, and track formative answer-building mastery by question without memorising scripts.
- **Speakia Coach and Writia** offer conversational support, guided response development, and learning activities that help learners shape and rehearse their ideas.
- **Vocabulary Studio and the Vocabulary Bank** connect topic prompts and useful expressions. Important vocabulary from AI-to-AI Simulation and Answer Builder sessions can be captured for future study.
- **Speakia Flashcards** support repeated retrieval of vocabulary, while memory estimates help learners decide what to revisit.
- **Media Assessments and Assignments** let learners work with recordings, video, text, or task submissions and review practice feedback.
- **Performance History** brings together saved sessions, transcripts, audio, and reports so a learner can reflect on change across attempts.
- **Speakia Podcasts** provide listening material and conversational examples that can feed into further discussion and practice.

Together, these modules form a loop rather than a set of isolated tools: examples and prompts lead to speaking; speaking creates evidence and useful language; reflection informs review; and review prepares the learner for another attempt.

## Product boundaries and trust

Speakia should be transparent that generated content and assessments are AI-assisted. Feedback is an aid to practice, may be imperfect, and must not be presented as an official IELTS score. Learners should be able to understand what they are sharing and how their local practice history is stored.

The current app is a browser-based prototype. API keys and practice data may be stored in browser storage, which is not encrypted application-secret storage. AI requests send relevant learner input to the configured service for processing. Do not use production, shared, billing-sensitive keys, or sensitive personal information in this prototype. A production product would need a secure server-side credential flow, explicit data and retention controls, and clear consent for recorded media.
